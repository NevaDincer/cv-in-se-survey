## Scope note

this paper's relevance to CV usage is questionable. The semantic matching process relies entirely on NLP-based word embedding techniques (Word2Vec, GloVe, FastText, BERT, NNLM, USE, Word Mover's Distance) and compares the textual attributes of GUI widgets (text, resource-id, content-desc, hint, neighbor-text, parent-text, sibling-text, activity-name, file-name). No visual or pixel-based technique, screenshot analysis, image embedding, OCR, is used as the paper's own contribution. CV approaches (DEEPGUI, Humanoid, VISTA, METER, GUIDER) only come up in the Related Work section, as a suggested direction for future research. It may fall outside the survey's CV-times-SE intersection; worth a deliberate include/exclude call.

## Summary

This paper runs the first systematic empirical study of how semantic matching components affect GUI Test Reuse for Android apps, a technique that migrates test cases from a source app to a functionally similar target app by finding semantically corresponding GUI events. The authors break the semantic matching pipeline shared by prior approaches (ATM, CraftDroid, AdaptDroid) into four components: corpus of documents, word embedding technique, event descriptor extractor, and semantic matching algorithm, then systematically vary each one. They also propose SemFinder, a new matching algorithm that concatenates all available textual widget attributes instead of restricting to a fixed subset, and scores similarity at the sentence level with average aggregation, which statistically outperforms ATM's, CraftDroid's, and AdaptDroid's own algorithms. Across 337 configurations tested on 8,099 GUI events, plus 68 configurations tested through two real test generators over 6,000+ migrations, the results show semantic embedding-based approaches clearly beating syntactic and random baselines, sentence-level embeddings beating word-level ones, and the event descriptor extractor mattering more to end-to-end effectiveness than the embedding algorithm itself. A domain-general corpus (Google Play app descriptions) mildly outperforms domain-specific or generic corpora. The most notable finding is a persistent gap between the best configurations and a perfect-matching upper bound, which the authors trace mainly to incompleteness of the target app's GUI model rather than to matching quality, pointing toward future work on many-to-many event mappings and, in passing, incorporating visual information alongside text.
