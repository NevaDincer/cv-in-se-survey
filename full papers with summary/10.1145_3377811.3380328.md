# Translating Video Recordings of Mobile App Usages into Replayable Scenarios (V2S)

- **Year:** 2020
- **Venue/Source:** ICSE '20 (42nd International Conference on Software Engineering), Seoul, Republic of Korea
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3377811.3380328

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Testing (GUI test/scenario generation, record-and-replay); also maintenance (bug reproduction from crowdsourced videos)
- **se_task:** Automatically converting screen-recording videos of Android app usage into replayable, executable GUI test scenarios (for bug reproduction, regression test creation, crowd-testing, demos)
- **cv_technique:** Faster R-CNN (VGGNet-based region proposal + classifier) for detecting/localizing the on-screen "touch indicator" in each video frame; a custom Opacity CNN (modified AlexNet) for classifying detected touch indicators as high- vs low-opacity (finger pressed vs. lifted) to segment discrete gestures
- **platform:** Mobile (Android; evaluated on Nexus 5 and Nexus 6P devices)
- **rationale:** Ease of use / low barrier to adoption (works from an unmodified, unrooted device using only the built-in "Show Touches" screen-recording feature, requiring no instrumentation, rooting, or custom OS); robustness (high mAP/precision/recall for touch detection and opacity classification across a wide range of native and non-native apps); context-driven (a graph-based grouping heuristic uses spatial distance and opacity between touches across consecutive frames to disambiguate overlapping/near-simultaneous gestures)
- **artifact_type:** Temporal (the core input/output unit is a sequence of video frames reconstructed into time-ordered gesture events (taps, long-taps, swipes) with millisecond-level timing derived from frame rate) combined with natural input (raw, unannotated screen-recording video as captured during ordinary device use)
- **eval_method:** mAP / mAP@.75 / Average Recall for the Faster R-CNN touch-detection model and the Opacity CNN (per-device, ~97-99%); Levenshtein distance and Longest Common Subsequence between generated and ground-truth action-type traces; precision/recall per action type (tap/long-tap/gesture); manual success-rate validation across 175 videos (64 from a controlled user study with 8 participants, 111 from a popular-apps study of 64 top Google Play apps); wall-clock performance measurement per phase; a qualitative case study with 3 industry developers (interviews + Likert-scale usefulness ratings)
- **challenges:** The Faster R-CNN model is tied to a specific device screen size and must be retrained per screen dimension; 30fps video is a lower bound and can fail to fully resolve very fast swipe/gesture sequences; generated replay scripts use raw (x,y) coordinates and frame timestamps rather than human-readable GUI-element metadata, making them hard to maintain (explicitly flagged as a limitation by interviewed developers); requires the device's "Show Touches" indicator to be visibly rendered and consistent; occasional false positives from the default touch indicator resembling other on-screen icons (mitigated with a custom high-contrast indicator, which requires a rooted device).

## Summary

V2S addresses a practical mobile-testing bottleneck: screen recordings are an easy, common way for users and testers to report bugs, but developers still have to watch and manually re-derive the steps by hand. V2S is framed as the first purely graphical Android record-and-replay technique, requiring nothing but a standard screen recording (with the built-in "Show Touches" indicator enabled) as input, with no rooted device and no custom instrumentation. The pipeline has three phases. Touch Detection uses a Faster R-CNN object detector (trained on a synthetically augmented version of the ReDraw screenshot dataset, with touch-indicator icons programmatically superimposed at varying opacity and screen positions) to localize the touch indicator in each frame, paired with a custom Opacity CNN (an adapted AlexNet) that classifies each detection as high- or low-opacity to signal finger-down vs. finger-lifting. Action Classification then models grouping of touches across frames as a graph-traversal problem, using spatial proximity and opacity transitions to split overlapping or rapid-fire touches into discrete taps, long-taps, or gestures. Scenario Generation converts these into low-level `sendevent` instructions replayed via a modified RERAN binary. Across 175 videos from 83 apps (a controlled 8-participant study plus a popular-apps study spanning 32 Google Play categories), V2S achieved ~95-99% precision/recall on touch and opacity detection and fully reproduced roughly 82-97% of scenarios depending on the dataset, with a notable finding from the industrial case study: developers wanted human-readable, GUI-metadata-based scripts (e.g., "tapped the send button") rather than raw pixel coordinates, and saw potential uses beyond bug reproduction, including QA automation, demo generation, and usage-analytics collection.
