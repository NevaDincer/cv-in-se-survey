# Automated Visual Testing for Mobile Apps in an Industrial Setting

- **Year:** 2022
- **Venue/Source:** 2022 IEEE/ACM 44th International Conference on Software Engineering: Software Engineering in Practice (ICSE-SEIP)
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3510457.3513027

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Testing
- **se_task:** Automated UI/GUI testing (exploration-based test generation) for industrial mobile apps, decomposed into two sub-tasks: UI-element identification from raw screenshots, and test-action planning (deciding what action to perform next).
- **cv_technique:** Multiple object-detection techniques evaluated and combined: Faster-RCNN (two-stage, anchor-based), YOLOv3 (one-stage, anchor-based), CenterNet (one-stage, anchor-free/keypoint-based), all deep-learning-based; Xianyu (old-fashioned CV: binarization, slicing, edge detection); UIED (old-fashioned CV for non-textual elements + deep-learning-based OCR via EAST for textual elements). The authors design an integrated technique running YOLOv3 and UIED in parallel and merging/filtering their outputs. Additional CV usage in the supporting infrastructure: OpenCV for background segmentation (isolating the device screen from its physical surroundings) and for camera calibration/marker (black-block) detection to project screenshot coordinates to real-world robotic-arm coordinates; perceptual hashing for screenshot similarity caching.
- **platform:** Mobile (Android and iOS primarily; motivated more broadly by cross-platform needs including AliOS and Harmony OS)
- **rationale:** Robustness (visual, screenshot-only identification can handle "non-standard" UI elements, e.g., embedded WebViews or game-engine-rendered UIs, whose internal structure platform accessibility APIs like UIAutomator cannot expose); context-driven (a case-by-case, "no one-size-fits-all" strategy is adopted: technique choice depends on whether a UI element has a clear vs. mixed boundary with its background and simple vs. rich internal structure); ease of use / cross-platform applicability (screenshot-only input requires no per-platform modification, reducing tool maintenance/learning cost); the paper also explicitly foregrounds monetary and runtime cost as first-class design considerations alongside these rationales.
- **artifact_type:** Full-interface (whole-screen UI-element identification from screenshots to support autonomous exploration across the app), combined with a physical/hardware artifact dimension via a robotic-arm + high-speed-camera infrastructure that treats the device screen itself as the interaction surface (fully non-intrusive, platform-agnostic execution).
- **eval_method:** (1) A controlled empirical study on 5 representative Taobao UI scenarios (Messenger, Goods detail, Live streaming, Shop Front, Game; 12 screenshots each) comparing 5 object-detection techniques via Precision/Recall/F1 against UIAutomator-derived ground truth at IoU thresholds of 0.5/0.75/0.95, plus per-technique processing-time measurements. (2) A system-level evaluation comparing VTest instantiated with each technique (and a combined + cached variant) against the industry-standard Monkey tool, measured via Android activity coverage and execution rounds over 6-hour runs repeated 3 times on a physical Huawei P30 device. (3) Large-scale industrial deployment reporting (internal use across ~8 Alibaba apps including Taobao and parts of Alipay; external deployment as a public service in the Software Green Alliance industry consortium).
- **challenges:** Deep-learning-based detectors generalize poorly across apps/UI variance even when trained on ~66,000 Rico screenshots, requiring costly app-specific fine-tuning (e.g., YOLOv3 mAP rose from 0.02 to 0.75 only after fine-tuning on 1,000 Taobao-specific screens) and creating ongoing maintenance burden as UIs evolve; anchor-based detectors (Faster-RCNN, YOLOv3) degrade sharply at high IoU thresholds (e.g., 0.95) because they assume fixed bounding-box scale/aspect ratio, unlike the anchor-free CenterNet; old-fashioned techniques (UIED, Xianyu) fail when UI elements are visually mixed with background imagery (e.g., live-streaming, game scenarios); no single technique dominates across all scenarios, motivating a hybrid combination; even the best techniques cap out around 60% detection accuracy, which constrains what test-action-planning strategies are feasible (the authors ultimately settle for a simple randomized strategy rather than more sophisticated model-based planning, since precise UI-hierarchy-dependent planning techniques are not robust to noisy visual detections); image processing is computationally expensive (up to 4.2s/screenshot for Faster-RCNN), requiring a perceptual-hashing cache to keep the system practical at scale; the hardware infrastructure (robotic arms, high-speed cameras) is costly to scale and is reserved for scenarios (e.g., low-end device testing, authentic user-experience testing) where the lower-cost, API-based software infrastructure is insufficient.

## Summary

This industry paper from Alibaba and Peking University reports the design, evaluation, and multi-year deployment of VTest, a visual testing framework built to satisfy two concrete industrial requirements that most academic UI-testing tools fail to meet: applicability across a proliferating set of mobile platforms (Android, iOS, AliOS, Harmony OS) and the ability to handle "non-standard" UI elements, such as embedded WebViews or game-engine-rendered widgets, whose internal structure cannot be retrieved via platform accessibility APIs like UIAutomator. VTest addresses this by treating UI testing purely as a computer-vision problem over device screenshots, decomposed into UI-element identification (framed as domain-specific object detection) and test-action planning. The paper's most substantial technical contribution is a systematic empirical comparison of five representative object-detection techniques: three deep-learning-based (Faster-RCNN, YOLOv3, CenterNet) and two "old-fashioned" CV techniques (Xianyu, and UIED augmented with deep-learning OCR), across five real Taobao UI scenarios of varying visual complexity, from which the authors derive a taxonomy of UI elements (by boundary clarity and internal-structure variety) explaining *why* different techniques excel in different scenarios, and design an integrated YOLOv3+UIED technique accordingly. Because sophisticated model-based test-action planning strategies require information (code coverage, element IDs) unavailable to a screenshot-only tool, and because even the best identification technique achieves only ~60% accuracy, VTest instead uses a simple randomized exploration strategy, and instead invests engineering effort in infrastructure: a dual hardware (robotic-arm + camera, using OpenCV for screen segmentation and camera-to-real-world coordinate calibration) and lightweight software (platform-API-based) infrastructure, plus a perceptual-hashing screenshot cache that alone contributed a 19.4 percentage-point activity-coverage improvement. Deployed at massive scale, testing Taobao (~1 billion monthly active users) and other Alibaba apps internally, and offered externally through the Software Green Alliance industry consortium spanning major Chinese device and app vendors, VTest outperformed the industry-standard Monkey tool by 87.6% in activity coverage while firing 90% fewer actions. The paper closes with five practitioner-oriented lessons learned (matching problems to solution costs, reusing tool modules across tasks, investigating individual cases rather than only averages, not dismissing non-deep-learning techniques, and treating infrastructure efficiency as equally important as algorithmic innovation) that are of particular interest for understanding how CV-based SE tools are adapted for real industrial constraints beyond raw accuracy.
