# It Takes Two to Tango: Combining Visual and Textual Information for Detecting Duplicate Video-Based Bug Reports

- **Year:** 2021
- **Venue/Source:** ICSE '21 (43rd IEEE/ACM International Conference on Software Engineering)
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ICSE43902.2021.00091

## Metadata

- **se_area:** maintenance (bug triaging / issue-tracker management)
- **se_task:** Duplicate video-based bug report detection: ranking a corpus of existing screen-recorded bug reports by similarity to a new incoming video report
- **cv_technique:** Two interchangeable visual feature extractors: SIFT (classical, local scale-invariant keypoint features) and SimCLR (self-supervised contrastive deep learning with a ResNet50 backbone, trained from scratch by the authors on the 66k-screenshot RICO dataset); features are aggregated into a video representation via Bag-of-Visual-Words (BoVW, using k-Means codebooks of 1k/5k/10k visual words) and TF-IDF encoding; two custom sequence-aware similarity algorithms, fuzzy-LCS (f-LCS) and weighted-LCS (w-LCS), an adapted longest-common-substring algorithm that fuzzy-matches frames and up-weights frames later in the video (where buggy behavior is likelier to appear); OCR (Tesseract) is used to extract on-screen text for the separate text-retrieval (Lucene/TF-IDF) component, which is linearly combined with the visual score
- **platform:** mobile (Android)
- **rationale:** context-driven (both LCS variants exploit video-specific temporal structure, deliberately weighting frames near the end of the recording more heavily because bug manifestation is more likely there); robustness (combining visual and textual modalities compensates for cases (e.g., high shared vocabulary between duplicate and non-duplicate videos) where one modality alone is unreliable, via a learned/derived weighting scheme); ease of use / practicality (operates purely on the graphical screen-recording itself, requiring no extra metadata, and the RICO-trained visual model generalizes to unseen apps not used in training)
- **artifact_type:** temporal (full video frame sequences are analyzed both order-insensitively, via whole-video BoVW/TF-IDF vectors, and order-sensitively, via frame-sequence LCS alignment that privileges temporally later frames)
- **eval_method:** Empirical evaluation on 4,860 duplicate-detection tasks derived from 180 screen-recordings (60 distinct bugs (10 real, plus mutation-injected via MutAPK) across 6 open-source Android apps, 3 duplicate videos per bug); effectiveness measured via mRR, mAP, mean rank, HIT@1/HIT@2 across four RQs isolating visual-only vs. textual-only (RQ1), ordered vs. unordered visual similarity (RQ2), and combined visual+textual configurations with a derived vocabulary-agreement-based weighting threshold (RQ3); a remote user study (RQ4) with 5 experienced participants (1 professional engineer, 4 CS PhD students) compared human manual duplicate-finding time/effort against TANGO's best configuration on 20 tasks.
- **challenges:** TANGOtxt's OCR+text-retrieval component is the least consistent component, degrading sharply for apps (TIME, TOK) where duplicate and non-duplicate videos share very similar on-screen vocabulary. The authors address this with an empirically-derived, non-generalizing vocabulary-agreement threshold (12.8%) to decide per-app whether to include textual information at all. Sequence-aware (LCS-based) visual similarity underperforms the simpler order-agnostic BoVW approach overall, which the authors attribute to LCS's restrictiveness (only the single longest common substring is considered) and to giving visual and sequential scores equal weight rather than tuning their combination. The visual TF-IDF background statistics are computed from the RICO screenshot dataset rather than from the actual video corpus, and from single images rather than frame groups, a generalization-motivated design choice the authors acknowledge as a limitation. Roughly 50 of the 60 studied bugs were injected via mutation testing (MutAPK) rather than being naturally occurring, and the approach's text-processing pipeline assumes English-language UI text, which the authors flag as unvalidated for multilingual bug reports.

## Scope note

None. Strong fit for the CV x SE survey. TANGO's visual component is substantive and central to the technique (not a superficial add-on): it directly compares two distinct CV feature-extraction paradigms (classical SIFT vs. a purpose-trained contrastive deep-learning model, SimCLR) and contributes a novel temporally-aware similarity algorithm (weighted-LCS) motivated by a software-engineering-specific insight (bugs manifest near the end of screen recordings). The paper also reports a rigorous ablation isolating the visual component's contribution from the textual component's, which is directly useful for synthesis-stage comparison against other CV×SE maintenance/testing papers in this batch.

## Summary

TANGO addresses duplicate detection for video-based (screen-recording) bug reports, an artifact type that, unlike textual bug reports, cannot be handled by existing duplicate-detection techniques due to its graphical nature. The approach frames duplicate detection as an information-retrieval ranking problem: given a new video-based report, TANGO scores its similarity against a corpus of previously submitted videos using two independently computable but linearly combinable components. TANGOvis extracts per-frame visual features using either SIFT or a self-supervised SimCLR model (trained by the authors on the RICO mobile-screenshot dataset to specialize it for app GUIs), aggregates them via a Bag-of-Visual-Words/TF-IDF pipeline for order-agnostic similarity, and additionally offers two order-sensitive variants (fuzzy-LCS and weighted-LCS) that align frame sequences and up-weight later frames, since buggy behavior typically appears toward the end of a recording. TANGOtxt extracts on-screen text via OCR and computes textual similarity using a standard Lucene/TF-IDF text-retrieval pipeline. The two scores are combined with a linear weight that the authors show can be predicted per-app from a "vocabulary agreement" heuristic: textual information should be down-weighted or excluded when duplicate and non-duplicate videos use very similar UI vocabulary. Evaluated on a purpose-built benchmark of 4,860 duplicate-detection tasks across six Android apps, TANGO's best (selectively-combined) configuration ranks the correct duplicate in the top 2 results for 83% of tasks, and a user study shows it can save developers roughly 65% of the time spent manually identifying duplicate video-based bug reports.
