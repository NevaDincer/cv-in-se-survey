# Vision-Based Mobile App GUI Testing: A Survey

- **Year:** 2025 (published online December 2025; search corpus collected through October 2024)
- **Venue/Source:** ACM Computing Surveys (ACM Comput. Surv.), Vol. 58, No. 6, Article 142, 46 pages. Authors: Shengcheng Yu, Chunrong Fang, Ziyuan Tuo, Quanjun Zhang, Zhenyu Chen (State Main Laboratory for Novel Software Technology, Nanjing University, China); Chunyang Chen (Monash University, Australia); Zhendong Su (ETH Zurich, Switzerland).
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3773027

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Testing (specifically mobile app GUI testing, spanning the full testing lifecycle: generation, execution/record-replay, maintenance, reporting, and evaluation).
- **se_task:** This is a secondary study (systematic survey) whose scope is the entire field of mobile app GUI testing, both non-vision-based and vision-based. It reviews 271 articles total, of which 92 are classified as vision-based studies. The survey organizes the field into three top-level categories and eight specific topics: (1) Automated GUI Testing: GUI Test Generation (GEN), GUI Test Record & Replay/Migration (R&R), GUI Testing Framework (FRM); (2) Automation-Assisted Manual GUI Testing: GUI Test Script Maintenance (SRP), GUI Test Report Analysis (RPT); (3) Fundamental Techniques for GUI Testing: GUI Element Detection (ELE), GUI Usability & Accessibility Testing (U&A), GUI Testing Evaluation Criteria (EVA). Within each topic, the survey traces how techniques evolved from code-based and layout-based approaches toward vision-based (image-based) approaches, and specifically highlights where and how CV techniques have reshaped each topic.
- **cv_technique:** The survey does not propose new CV techniques itself but catalogs the range used across the 92 vision-based studies it reviews, including: image differencing / image processing for GUI comparison and replay-error detection; traditional CV feature/pattern matching (e.g., Sikuli's screenshot-pattern-based GUI scripting); optical character recognition (OCR) for extracting widget text; object detection and image classification for identifying and classifying GUI widgets/user actions in screenshots or screen-recording video frames (e.g., V2S); convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for GUI element/component classification (e.g., Sun et al., OwlEye); deep learning-based widget/display-issue detection (visual GUI bug and display-issue detection, e.g., OwlEye, Chen et al.'s widget detector); unsupervised CV-based adversarial autoencoders for GUI animation/linting checks (Zhao et al.); CV combined with natural language generation for describing GUI changes across app releases (Moran et al.); CV-based icon/sensitive-content categorization (Xiao et al.); non-invasive CV for extracting touchscreen actions from human demonstration videos (RoScript); GAN-based GUI design/prototype generation (GUIGAN); and, in the challenges/future-directions section, emerging multimodal large language models (LLM/vision-language models, e.g., GPT-4(Vision)) for GUI screenshot captioning, semantic understanding, and multimodal fusion of visual and textual GUI information.
- **platform:** Mobile (Android and iOS apps are the primary focus; some cross-platform work spanning mobile-to-web or mobile-to-desktop migration is also discussed, and one referenced study addresses mobile game GUI testing).
- **rationale:** The survey explains that CV/vision-based approaches emerged to overcome fundamental limitations of code- and layout-based GUI testing: (1) the mobile "fragmentation problem" (many device/OS/version combinations) makes structural approaches hard to generalize, whereas GUI screenshots tend to look visually similar across environments; (2) layout/accessibility files often omit or misrepresent GUI elements (e.g., custom Canvas-drawn widgets, dynamically refreshed content), causing inaccurate widget identification and unfaithful simulation of user actions; (3) vision-based analysis mimics how human testers perceive "what you see is what you get," aligning with the mental-model basis of GUI design; (4) most real-world testing is black-box (apps distributed as .apk with no source access), so image-based analysis of screenshots is often the only accessible signal. In short, the rationale is robustness/generalizability across fragmented environments, coverage of elements invisible to structural analysis, and closer fidelity to actual human perception of the GUI.
- **artifact_type:** The survey covers the full range: full-interface (whole-screen screenshot/GUI-page analysis for element detection, display-issue/bug detection, and GUI design/prototype generation); localized (detection/localization of individual widgets/icons/buttons within a screenshot, including sensitive-icon classification and widget region detection in domain-specific apps); temporal (screen-recording video analysis and action-sequence extraction for record & replay/migration, e.g., converting demonstration videos or screen-record frames into replayable test scripts: V2S, RoScript, GIFdroid); and natural input (video/demonstration-based capture of human touchscreen interactions as the basis for generating test scripts).
- **eval_method:** The survey itself (not the reviewed primary studies) used a systematic literature review methodology: (1) explicit inclusion criteria (articles introducing GUI-testing concepts/challenges, proposing GUI-testing frameworks/tools/methodologies, presenting GUI-testing datasets/benchmarks, or offering directly assistive technologies) and exclusion of general/backend testing work without GUI relevance; (2) keyword-based database search on DBLP and Google Scholar (search executed October 2024) using three compound Boolean queries combining terms like "GUI"/"mobile app"/"Android"/"iOS"/"testing"/"screenshot"/"image"; (3) automated metadata extraction and deduplication via a web crawler; (4) venue-based filtering to six high-impact journals and fourteen prominent SE/PL/HCI conferences; (5) manual validation and exclusion of unpublished preprints; (6) snowballing through related-work sections and reference lists of retrieved articles to capture additional relevant studies. This produced a final corpus of 271 articles (92 vision-based). Three authors independently labeled each article's "source code requirement" (black-box/grey-box/white-box) and "approach basis" (code/layout/image/combinations); disagreements were resolved by two senior authors (15+ years' experience) reaching consensus, and inter-rater reliability was measured with Fleiss' kappa = 0.95 ("almost perfect agreement"). The full article list is published in an accompanying online repository for replication.
- **challenges:** The survey dedicates a full section (Section 6) to challenges and opportunities specific to vision-based GUI testing, organized into five themes: (1) GUI element detection and semantic understanding: difficulty adapting recognition to varied frameworks/platforms/resolutions, handling dynamic/asynchronous/occluded elements, image-quality sensitivity causing mismatches, heavy data/compute needs of AI-based recognition, and integration friction with existing testing tools; proposed directions include multimodal fusion and knowledge-graph-based semantic modeling. (2) Oracle generation: traditional CV/DL models tend to suppress subtle, buggy GUI elements as "noise," making bug-oracle generation hard; proposed directions include semantic- and context-aware oracle generation, adaptive oracles that learn from historical results/user interaction, incorporating user feedback, and cross-platform-compatible oracles. (3) Expert domain knowledge integration: difficulty acquiring, representing, and consistently applying domain expertise (ontologies/rules/cases) across multiple domains and levels; proposed directions include knowledge graphs, collaborative expert platforms, and version control over evolving knowledge bases. (4) App scenario identification, segmentation, combination, and understanding: combinatorial explosion of scenarios across users/devices/operations, dynamic/uncertain scenario boundaries, and coverage difficulty in large complex apps; proposed directions combine CV with NLP and data mining for automatic scenario identification, dynamic (e.g., reinforcement-learning-based) segmentation, and cross-scenario test suites. (5) Large language model (LLM) utilization: LLM outputs are complex/hard to interpret automatically, pretrained generality misaligns with domain-specific testing needs, and matching LLM output to expected GUI behavior for test-case generation is non-trivial; opportunities include LLM-based test script/report generation, multimodal (vision-language) fusion for detecting layout/color/font issues directly from screenshots (demonstrated with GPT-4(Vision) captioning), and adaptive, personalized testing.

## Summary

This ACM Computing Surveys article (46 pages, Dec. 2025) is a systematic secondary study of mobile app GUI testing that specifically foregrounds the rise of vision-based (image/screenshot-driven) techniques as a successor to code- and layout-based approaches. Built from a rigorously constructed corpus of 271 articles (92 vision-based) gathered via DBLP/Google Scholar keyword search, venue filtering, and snowballing, the survey organizes the field into eight topics under three umbrella categories: automated GUI testing (test generation, record & replay/migration, testing frameworks), automation-assisted manual testing (script maintenance, report analysis), and fundamental techniques (element detection, usability/accessibility testing, evaluation criteria). Each topic is cross-cut by two taxonomies, source-code requirement (black/grey/white-box) and approach basis (code/layout/image/combinations), coded independently by three authors with a Fleiss' kappa of 0.95, an unusually rigorous inter-rater-reliability step for an SE secondary study. A notable statistic: across all 271 studies, 83 use image-only approaches versus 102 code-only and 71 layout-only, indicating vision-based methods already rival traditional bases in this literature, and the ELE (element detection) topic is overwhelmingly vision-based (36 of 39 articles). The survey's dedicated "Challenges and Opportunities" section is its most forward-looking contribution, identifying five open problems specific to vision-based mobile GUI testing: element detection/semantic understanding, oracle generation, expert-knowledge integration, scenario identification/segmentation, and LLM/multimodal-vision-language-model integration, closing with a demonstration of GPT-4(Vision)'s screenshot-captioning ability as a preview of where the field is heading. As a survey, this paper contributes no new CV technique of its own; it is being catalogued here purely as a related-work / meta-source mapping the vision-based mobile GUI testing sub-literature that a broader CV-in-SE review should be aware of.
