# Enhancing GUI Exploration Coverage of Android Apps with Deep Link-Integrated Monkey

## Summary

Delm (Deep Link-enhanced Monkey) is an Android GUI testing tool that augments existing dynamic exploration testers (built on Monkey) with Android deep links to overcome two persistent problems in automated GUI testing: activities buried deep in the navigation hierarchy or behind obscure UI interactions, and testers becoming trapped in repetitive exploration loops among a small set of common screens. Delm operates in two phases: a static analysis phase that extracts each activity's required Inter-Component Communication (ICC) contexts (attributes and extra parameters) via def-use chain analysis over Java/smali code, builds an Activity Transition Graph (ATG) using IC3 and Soot, and extracts or injects deep links into the app manifest; and a dynamic exploration phase where a loop-detection algorithm monitors GUI states (hashed from the GUI hierarchy tree) and, upon detecting a stuck loop or timeout, guides the tester via deep-link-triggered activity launches (with carefully mocked-up contexts) toward unexplored adjacent activities in the ATG, while conservatively refusing to mock ambiguous contexts to avoid false-positive crashes. Its main contribution is this rigorous, false-positive-averse context mock-up and deep-link-integrated guided exploration strategy, evaluated on IntentBench, IndustrialApps, Themis, AndroTest, and Google Play industrial apps, achieving substantial gains in activity coverage (27.2%), method coverage (21.13%), and crash detection (23.81%) over state-of-the-art baselines. A distinctive detail is the tool's deliberate conservatism: it explicitly declines to mock ambiguous or wildcard implicit-intent contexts specifically to eliminate false-positive crashes, trading some reachability for correctness. Notably, the approach is entirely based on structural/static program analysis; the authors state computer vision techniques are reserved for future work only.
