# Classification of UML Diagrams to Support Software Engineering Education

- **Year:** 2021
- **Venue/Source:** ASEW 2021
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ASEW52652.2021.00030

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Requirements
- **se_task:** Multi-class classification of UML diagram images into 6 diagram types (class, use case, sequence, component, activity, deployment) to enable accessible/assistive didactic materials for visually impaired or blind SE students
- **cv_technique:** CNN transfer learning (VGG16, InceptionV3, ResNet50 pre-trained on ImageNet); data augmentation (horizontal/vertical flip, +/-90 degree rotation); three transfer-learning strategies (full fine-tune, partial layer freeze, frozen convolutional base used as feature extractor)
- **platform:** General
- **rationale:** Context-driven (accessibility motivation: screen readers/text cannot describe diagram images); Ease of use (transfer learning chosen specifically to cope with small labeled datasets rather than training from scratch)
- **artifact_type:** Document (UML diagram images collected via web scraping plus the Lindholmen Dataset)
- **eval_method:** Experiment 1: 5-fold cross-validation on a small dataset (200 imgs/class) with Strategy 3 (frozen conv base); Experiment 2: 10-fold cross-validation on an augmented dataset (1000 imgs/class) with Strategy 2 (partial unfreeze); Experiment 3: held-out test set (50 imgs/class, disjoint from training) evaluated with the best model (InceptionV3, augmented data, Strategy 2), reported via confusion matrix
- **challenges:** Class diagrams are markedly harder to classify (72% test accuracy) than the other five types (>=90%), most often confused with deployment diagrams, due to high diversity of association types and the fact that class-diagram associations' visual representation varies with class layout position; overall lack of large public UML-diagram datasets forced the authors to build their own via web scraping + augmentation; horizontal/vertical flip augmentation may actually be harmful for the semantics of some diagram types (noted as a lesson learned, not fully resolved)

## Summary

This paper targets a gap a prior systematic literature review identified: while several works make STEM diagrams accessible for blind and low-vision students, few address UML at all, and those that do restrict themselves to binary class-diagram-versus-not classification. The authors instead build and release a 6-class UML dataset, class, use case, sequence, component, activity, deployment, 200 images per class collected through Google and Bing scraping plus the public Lindholmen dataset, and benchmark three well-known CNN backbones (VGG16, InceptionV3, ResNet50) under transfer learning, comparing three fine-tuning strategies: training everything, training some layers while freezing others, and freezing the convolutional base entirely. Across both a small and an augmented (6,000-image, 4x augmentation via flips and rotations) version of the dataset, InceptionV3 wins consistently, 87.8% on the small dataset with a fully frozen conv base and 98.7% on the augmented one with 175 unfrozen layers, beating ResNet50 and VGG16 in both scenarios, and augmentation improves every architecture. The held-out test, using the best configuration (InceptionV3, augmented data, partial unfreeze), generalizes well for five of six diagram types (90% or higher, several above 98%) but drops sharply for class diagrams (72%), with the confusion matrix pointing to systematic misclassification as deployment diagrams, attributed to class diagrams' greater structural complexity and to association visuals shifting depending on class box placement. The paper positions this as a first step toward a full assistive-technology pipeline, with future work aimed at diagram-specific augmentation tuning and eventual OCR-based content extraction, not just type classification.
