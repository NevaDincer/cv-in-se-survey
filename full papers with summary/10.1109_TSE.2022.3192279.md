# Translating Video Recordings of Complex Mobile App UI Gestures into Replayable Scenarios

- **Year:** 2023 (published online 25 July 2022; issue dated April 2023)
- **Venue/Source:** IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering (TSE), Vol. 49, No. 4, April 2023
- **DOI:** 10.1109/TSE.2022.3192279

## Metadata

- **se_area:** testing (with relevance to maintenance/bug reporting)
- **se_task:** Translating screen-recording videos of Android app usages (depicting single- and multi-fingered touch gestures) into replayable test/bug-reproduction scenarios: automated GUI-based record-and-replay purely from screen-recording video, without access to app source code, instrumentation, or GUI metadata.
- **cv_technique:** Faster R-CNN object detector (with a VGGNet backbone pre-trained on MS-COCO) adapted to localize a "touch indicator" (the on-screen circle Android's "Show Touches" feature renders) in each video frame; a custom Opacity CNN (a modified AlexNet architecture) that classifies each detected touch-indicator crop as high- or low-opacity to determine finger-down vs. finger-lifted state; plus a non-DL heuristic/graph-based post-processing pipeline (touch grouping via a graph-connectivity algorithm, action segmentation, and single-/multi-fingered action identification) that operates on the CV models' outputs to reconstruct discrete gestures (Tap, Long Tap, Gesture, Multi-Fingered Gesture).
- **platform:** mobile (Android; explicitly supports both native and hybrid, web+native, Android apps)
- **rationale:** Context-driven and robustness-driven. The authors explicitly argue that traditional image-processing techniques (e.g., Canny edge detection, color analysis) are not robust because they require manually tuned thresholds that do not generalize across the widely varying contrast/visual content of different apps' screens; DL-based CV (Faster R-CNN + custom CNN) is chosen specifically to be robust to this variability without per-app/per-image parameter tuning. Ease-of-use is also a strong driver: the approach is designed to require only a plain screen recording as input (no rooted device, no instrumentation, no GUI metadata), making it a low-barrier, "frictionless" tool for crowdsourced/beta testing and bug reporting.
- **artifact_type:** temporal (the technique tracks touch indicators across sequences of video frames to reconstruct gesture timing/duration) combined with localized (bounding-box detection of the touch indicator's spatial location per frame); the final output is a natural input replay (low-level sendevent touch commands reproducing the original finger gestures on a real/emulated device).
- **eval_method:** Multi-part empirical evaluation with 6 RQs. (1) Faster R-CNN detection accuracy evaluated via mAP (COCO-style, IoU 0.5–0.95) and Average Recall on a synthetically generated dataset (15k images, 70/30 train/test split) built by superimposing touch-indicator icons onto REDRAW dataset screenshots; separate models trained per device (Nexus 5, Nexus 6P), for both a default and a custom high-contrast touch indicator, achieving ≥97% mAP and ≥98% recall. (2) Opacity CNN evaluated via confusion matrix/precision on a 10k-image dataset (70/30 split), reaching 98.2%-99.4% precision. (3) End-to-end replay accuracy (RQ3, single-fingered actions) evaluated on 243 real user-recorded videos across 90+ native and hybrid apps (Controlled Study: 64 videos/20 apps with 8 human participants reproducing bugs/crashes/normal usage; Popular Applications Study: 111 scenarios/60 apps; Hybrid Study: 53 videos/14 hybrid apps with 7 participants) using Levenshtein distance, Longest Common Subsequence (LCS), precision/recall on a "bag of actions," and manually verified per-action success rate against human-derived ground truth. (4) Multi-fingered actions (RQ4) evaluated on 12 videos (44 MFAs, 71 SFAs) from 4 apps in 2 categories (Art & Design, Navigation), with artificial and natural scenario variants, same metrics. (5) Overhead/performance (RQ5) measured as seconds-per-frame on an NVIDIA GTX 1080Ti across the 3 pipeline phases. (6) Perceived usefulness (RQ6) assessed via semi-structured interviews and Likert-scale questions with 3 industrial developers comparing manually executed vs. V2S+-replayed scenarios. Main results: 90.2% of sequential native-app actions and 83% of hybrid-app actions correctly reproduced; 93.75%/81.98% of controlled-study/popular-app native scenarios fully reproduced; multi-fingered scenarios reproduced at 83.3% (scenario level), 88.2% LCS; ~105 minutes to process a 3-minute video on commodity hardware; developers rated V2S+ as accurate and potentially useful.
- **challenges:** Authors explicitly report: (1) the Faster R-CNN model is tied to a specific device screen size, requiring a separately trained model per screen resolution (mitigated by fully automated training, but adds setup cost); (2) generated replay scripts are tied to specific screen dimensions and are not human-readable (coordinate-based rather than semantic); (3) replay fidelity for fast swipes/gestures is limited by video frame rate. 30fps was insufficient to fully resolve some very fast gestures, and the authors recommend ≥60fps; (4) performance is noticeably worse on emulated devices than physical devices, because mouse/trackpad-driven interaction on an emulator produces "jagged"/unnatural touch trajectories and emulator lag degrades video quality, which was compounded by COVID-19-driven remote/emulator-only data collection for the hybrid study; (5) threats to validity acknowledged include manual/subjective validation of replay correctness (mitigated via dual-author verification), potential bias in author-recorded evaluation videos, and limited generalizability of the 3-developer usefulness interviews. Developers also requested (as a limitation of the current design) semantic/GUI-metadata-based scripts instead of raw coordinates, and annotated/explanatory output videos, both left as future work.

## Summary

This paper presents V2S+, an automated, purely computer-vision-based record-and-replay approach that converts screen-recording videos of Android app usage into replayable test/bug-reproduction scenarios, requiring only a video with Android's built-in "Show Touches" indicator enabled, with no app instrumentation, rooted device, or source access needed. V2S+ operates in three phases: Touch Detection, which uses a Faster R-CNN (VGGNet backbone) to localize the touch-indicator circle in every video frame and a custom Opacity CNN (modified AlexNet) to classify each detection as finger-down or finger-lifted; Action Classification, which uses heuristic/graph-based grouping algorithms to segment touch sequences into discrete actions (Tap, Long Tap, Gesture) and to separate and classify single- vs. multi-fingered actions (supporting up to ten fingers); and Scenario Generation, which converts classified actions into low-level `sendevent` commands replayed via a modified RERAN engine. A main contribution is a fully automated dataset-generation and training pipeline that synthesizes labeled training images by superimposing touch indicators onto the existing REDRAW screenshot corpus, avoiding manual annotation. V2S+ is a substantial extension of the ICSE'20 V2S paper, reimplemented entirely in Python (from a Java/Bash/Python mix) with a modular, extensible architecture, and newly adds support for multi-fingered gesture recognition and replay. Evaluated on 243 real user-recorded videos (4,028 actions) across 90+ native and hybrid apps, V2S+ reproduces 90.2% of sequential native-app actions and 83% of hybrid-app actions, with a case study showing three industrial developers perceived it as accurate and useful for bug reproduction and app demos.
