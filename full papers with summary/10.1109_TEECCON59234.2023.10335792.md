# An Advanced Multimodal Navigation Perception System for the Visually Impaired

> **SCOPE WARNING**: This paper is an embedded-hardware/assistive-technology project (a GPS/GSM/ultrasonic-sensor "smart blind stick"), not a software engineering study. Despite the abstract listing "computer vision" among the technologies it claims to integrate, no computer vision, image processing, or camera-based component is actually present anywhere in the described methodology, circuit design, simulation, or results. The entire system (Sections III–V) is built from five ultrasonic distance sensors, a soil/water sensor, GPS, GSM, a push-button, a buzzer, a vibration motor, and an Arduino Mega, all validated only via Proteus circuit simulation. "Computer vision" and "haptic feedback" appear once in the abstract as buzzwords and are never operationalized, tested, or even referenced again in the rest of the paper. This paper should very likely be EXCLUDED from a "CV in Software Engineering" survey. It has no genuine CV technique and only a nominal, unsubstantiated SE connection (it is framed as an interdisciplinary "software engineering, gadget design, and health science" project, but the actual contribution is electronics/embedded-systems design, not software engineering practice or CV method).

## Summary

This paper presents a low-cost "smart stick" (framed as an "Advanced Multimodal Navigation Perception System") intended to help visually impaired users navigate independently. The device integrates five ultrasonic sensors (front, left, right, and up/down for stair detection) to detect obstacles and stairs within roughly 20–450 cm, a soil/water sensor to detect puddles or wet ground, a GPS module and GSM module to track and transmit the user's location to a caregiver via a push-button "help" signal (with live tracking shown through the Blynk mobile app), and buzzer/vibration/voice output to alert the user. The system is built around an Arduino Mega microcontroller programmed in C. The paper's main claimed contribution is an affordable, portable, low-power assistive device combining multiple sensing modalities (ultrasonic ranging, GPS/GSM localization, water detection, and fall detection) into a single walking stick, evaluated only through Proteus circuit simulation rather than physical prototyping or user studies. Simulated outputs are shown for each sensor threshold condition, GSM emergency messaging, GPS tracking, and a "Sudden Fall detection" feature via the Blynk app. A notable detail is that although the abstract explicitly lists "computer vision" as one of the integrated technologies, the actual system, methodology, and all reported results are built entirely from non-visual ultrasonic/GPS/GSM sensing. No camera-based or image-processing component appears anywhere in the implementation, making the "computer vision" claim unsubstantiated within the paper itself.
