# Unblind Your Apps: Predicting Natural-Language Labels for Mobile GUI Components by Deep Learning

- **Year:** 2020
- **Venue/Source:** ICSE 2020 (42nd International Conference on Software Engineering), Seoul, Republic of Korea
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3377811.3380327

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Maintenance (accessibility-focused software maintenance/quality improvement); also touches development (proactive tooling for developers)
- **se_task:** Automatically predicting natural-language content-description labels for image-based GUI buttons (icon buttons, image buttons) in Android apps to support screen-reader (TalkBack) accessibility for visually impaired users, addressing the widespread developer failure to supply such labels
- **cv_technique:** ResNet-101 (pretrained on MS COCO) as a CNN visual feature extractor, feeding a Transformer encoder-decoder (multi-head self-attention encoder + cross-attention decoder, image-captioning style) that generates the label word sequence; compared against CNN+LSTM and CNN+CNN (fully convolutional decoder) baselines using the same CNN encoder
- **platform:** Mobile (Android)
- **rationale:** Robustness/context-driven (large-scale empirical motivation study empirically demonstrating the severity and pervasiveness of the missing-label problem: 77.38% of 10,408 sampled apps have at least one unlabeled image-based button) and ease of use (proactive, automated tool intended to replace reliance on developers manually and inconsistently adding accessibility labels)
- **artifact_type:** Localized (operates on individually cropped image-based button elements extracted from full GUI screenshots via their XML bounding boxes, not the whole screen)
- **eval_method:** (1) Large-scale empirical/motivational study crawling 19,127 apps and analyzing 278,234 screens to quantify missing-label prevalence by app category and installation count (Spearman correlation test); (2) construction of a training/testing dataset of 19,233 image-button/label pairs from 7,594 apps after deduplication and noise filtering; (3) quantitative accuracy evaluation on a held-out 10% test set using exact-match rate plus BLEU@1-4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, and CIDEr, with Mann-Whitney U tests (Benjamini-Hochberg corrected, p<0.01) against the two baselines; (4) qualitative error analysis comparing correctly vs. incorrectly predicted examples across methods; (5) a generalization/usefulness user study on 12 unseen apps (156 missing-label buttons) comparing LabelDroid's predictions against three human junior-developer annotators, scored blindly by an independent professional accessibility evaluator using a 5-point Likert "acceptability score," with sanity-check trap cases inserted to validate evaluator diligence
- **challenges:** Author-reported failure causes from manual analysis of 5% of wrong predictions: (1) unusual/atypical input buttons very different from training distribution (e.g., composite icons blending multiple app logos, or visually similar icons like "route" vs. "next" arrows leading to confusion); (2) semantically valid alternative labels that differ lexically from ground truth but convey equivalent meaning (e.g., predicting "open navigation drawer" for ground truth "menu"), suggesting true accuracy is underestimated; (3) a small number of cases where the ground truth itself was mislabeled by original developers. Also noted: baselines (CNN+LSTM, CNN+CNN) struggle with noisy/colorful backgrounds and long-sequence labels, while the Transformer-based model is comparatively more robust to background noise and better at capturing fine-grained visual differences (e.g., arrow direction) and long dependency structures via cross-attention.

## Summary

This paper addresses a large-scale, empirically-motivated accessibility problem: image-based buttons in Android apps frequently lack the natural-language content-description labels that screen readers (e.g., TalkBack) need to serve visually impaired users. The authors first conduct a motivational study crawling 19,127 apps, finding that 77.38% of 10,408 apps with image-based buttons have at least one unlabeled instance, with the problem worse in visually-oriented categories (personalization, games, photography) and showing no meaningful correlation with app popularity. To address this, they propose LabelDroid, which treats label generation as an image-captioning problem: a ResNet-101 CNN (pretrained on MS COCO) extracts visual features from a cropped button image, which a Transformer encoder-decoder (multi-head self-attention plus cross-attention) converts into a natural-language label, trained via KL-divergence loss on a curated dataset of 19,233 image-button/label pairs mined from 7,594 apps. On a held-out test set, LabelDroid achieves 65.0% exact-match accuracy, significantly outperforming CNN+LSTM and CNN+CNN baselines by 2-11.3% across BLEU/METEOR/ROUGE-L/CIDEr metrics (p<0.01). A notable detail is the generalization user study: on 156 missing-label buttons from 12 entirely unseen apps, LabelDroid's blindly-rated acceptability score (3.97 on a 5-point scale) significantly exceeded those of three human junior Android developers (3.06-3.62), with rigor ensured via inserted sanity-check trap labels for the independent evaluator. This suggests the automated model can outperform typical developer effort on this accessibility task.
