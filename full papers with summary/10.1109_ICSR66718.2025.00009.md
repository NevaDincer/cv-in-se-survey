# Exploiting Vision-Language Models in GUI Reuse

- **Year:** 2025
- **Venue/Source:** ICSR 2025 (IEEE/ACM 22nd International Conference on Software and Systems Reuse)
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ICSR66718.2025.00009

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Development / Requirements (GUI prototyping and requirements elicitation via rapid, creative reuse of existing GUI designs)
- **se_task:** GUI-centered software reuse: recommending which natural-language "reuse direction" (keyphrase) and which specific focused GUI element a designer should target when repurposing an existing GUI screenshot to create a new prototype, as a way to reduce "cognitive distance" and support creative ideation during GUI prototyping
- **cv_technique:** Vision-Language Models (Qwen2-VL-7B and Llama-Vision-11B) prompted to (1) select the top-matching reuse-direction keyphrase for a given GUI screenshot from a candidate list, and (2) identify which region/element of the image corresponds to that keyphrase (the "focused GUI element"); a pretrained image diffusion model used generatively to produce AI-based GUI-reuse variants by inpainting/modifying masked regions of GUI screenshots (masking based on the focused element identified by the VLM); compared against a non-VLM, non-CV natural-language baseline (RaWi's BERT-based textual-profile ranking, combined with XUI's deep-learning-based salient-element captioning)
- **platform:** Mobile/App GUI (GUI screenshots sourced from the Rico mobile-app-design repository, used by both the RaWi and XUI prior-work datasets)
- **rationale:** Context-driven (VLMs and diffusion models process the actual visual content of a GUI screenshot together with natural-language keyphrases to recommend contextually relevant reuse directions and generate visually coherent AI-modified variants); ease of use / creativity support (aims to reduce "cognitive distance" for human designers by pinpointing a specific, actionable element to edit rather than requiring them to search the entire GUI for what to change, thereby better supporting creative ideation)
- **artifact_type:** Full-interface (whole GUI screenshot given as VLM/diffusion input) combined with localized (a specific "focused GUI element" region identified and later masked/edited)
- **eval_method:** Empirical study with 73 university students performing 10 GUI-reuse tasks (5 tasks per participant, pen-and-paper prototyping, no digital aids) comparing reuse directions recommended by two VLMs, an NL-based (RaWi+XUI) method, and a random baseline, analyzed via Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (with effect sizes) on which recommendations participants actually followed, plus χ² tests relating "amount of reuse" (little reuse / GUI-change-only / GUI-keyphrase co-change) to whether changes targeted the focused vs. non-focused element; separate creativity assessment where 4 experienced UX/design experts ranked human-reuse vs. AI-diffusion-reuse results (measured via Kendall's τ inter-rater agreement)
- **challenges:** VLMs significantly outperformed the NL-based (RaWi+XUI) method in recommendation-following, but surprisingly the recommended "focused" GUI element was not consistently what participants actually modified (about 40% of participants who changed the GUI edited non-focused elements instead), and this focused-vs-non-focused difference was not statistically/practically significant (χ² p=0.139, small Cramér's V); the NL-based baseline unexpectedly performed even worse than pure random recommendations, a "puzzling" result the authors could not fully explain (hypothesized causes include overly broad/complex keyphrases and untrustworthy recommenders actively distracting users); in the creativity assessment, human-generated reuse results were still judged more creative overall than AI-diffusion-generated results (24 vs. 16 "most creative" rankings), suggesting current generative-AI reuse has not yet matched human designers on creative/clever reuse; results may not generalize beyond the specific dataset, VLMs, NL-baseline, and student-participant population used

## Summary

This paper investigates whether vision-language models (VLMs) can help software developers and designers reuse existing GUI designs more creatively, addressing a gap in prior GUI-reuse research that focused mainly on retrieving relevant candidate GUIs but offered little support for the actual act of reusing (editing) a chosen GUI. The authors frame GUI-centered reuse as: given a single existing GUI screenshot, a single GUI corresponds to multiple different natural-language "keyphrases," each representing a distinct plausible direction in which that GUI could be repurposed (e.g., a login screen's GUI might be reused toward "country code listing" or "weekday digest for meal planning"). They use two VLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B and Llama-Vision-11B) to recommend, for a given GUI, both a top-matching reuse-direction keyphrase and a specific "focused GUI element" that should be the starting point for edits, intended to reduce a psychological "cognitive distance" barrier that inhibits productive reuse. These VLM-based recommendations are compared against an NL-based baseline (built from prior work RaWi and XUI) and a random baseline in a controlled empirical study with 73 university students performing pen-and-paper GUI reuse tasks, finding that VLMs were followed far more often than the NL-based method (over 70% of the time) with large statistically significant effect sizes, while the NL-based method surprisingly performed even worse than random recommendations. However, the paper's more nuanced finding is that the specific "focused element" VLMs recommended as an edit target was not reliably what human designers actually changed. About 40% of participants instead modified other, non-focused parts of the GUI, indicating that pinpointing a location does not fully solve the creative-reuse problem. As a further generative extension, the authors used a diffusion model to produce AI-generated reuse variants (masking either the focused or non-focused element per the VLM's identification) and had four experienced UX designers rank the creativity of human-drawn versus AI-diffusion-generated reuse results, finding that human-produced reuse sketches were still judged the most creative more often than AI-generated ones, leading the authors to propose a human-AI collaborative "GUI ideation framework" (Fig. 11) in which VLM/diffusion recommendations serve as scaffolding for, rather than a replacement of, human creative judgment.
