# Layout and Image Recognition Driving Cross-Platform Automated Mobile Testing

- **Year:** 2021
- **Venue/Source:** 2021 IEEE/ACM 43rd International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE 2021), pp. 1561–1571
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ICSE43902.2021.00139

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Testing
- **se_task:** Cross-platform automated GUI test script record-and-replay: record a test script once on one device/platform and non-intrusively replay it on other platforms (Android, iOS; the framework is also motivated by mobile web and WeChat mini-programs, though the empirical evaluation covers Android and iOS only)
- **cv_technique:** Two complementary matching algorithms, combined with a tunable weight γ: (1) **Widget Feature Matching**, SIFT feature extraction on grayscale-preprocessed widget/activity screenshots, FLANN (KD-Tree index + KNN) approximate nearest-neighbor feature matching between target-widget and activity-screenshot feature sets, Lowe's ratio test (threshold δ=0.5) for mismatch elimination, and homography-based perspective transformation to correct for rotation/zoom distortion and recover final widget coordinates; (2) **Layout Characterization Matching**, Canny edge detection + dilation/corrosion (morphological ops) + `findContours()`-based contour extraction on the activity screenshot, followed by a custom Group→Line→Column segmentation producing a platform-independent 3-tuple (g,l,c) position encoding per widget; small contours (<1% of screenshot area) are discarded as noise, and OCR-extracted widget text is used as an auxiliary disambiguation signal
- **platform:** Mobile (Android and iOS empirically evaluated; framework also targets mobile web and app-embedded mini-programs like WeChat)
- **rationale:** Robustness (DOM/XPath/widget-ID-based locators break across platforms and even across OS versions/device models on the *same* platform. The paper's core empirical premise, validated by checking that 8/10 top free and 7/10 top paid Google Play apps have cross-platform versions with highly similar UI layouts, is that visual/layout similarity survives where code-level implementation similarity does not); Ease of use (fully non-intrusive: no source code, no APK instrumentation, no platform-specific test framework knowledge required; realizes "one script record, multiple script replays")
- **artifact_type:** Hybrid. Localized (cropped widget screenshots, `SS_w`) used for Widget Feature Matching, combined with Full-interface (whole activity screenshot, `SS_a`) used for Layout Characterization Matching and overall activity context
- **eval_method:** Empirical study using the LIRAT tool, built on a novel platform-independent test script model called LITS (Layout & Image Test Script, a list of 7-tuples `<ID, SS_a, SS_w, O, C_wr, C_or, T>`). 21 real-world apps (11 open-source, 10 commercial) across 10 categories (system, music, internet, phone&SMS, development, finance, tool, sports, shopping, map); 4 senior SE students designed 105 test scenarios (15 operations each) recorded on one Android device (Huawei Honor 8X Max) and replayed simultaneously on 5 Android devices (Oppo, Xiaomi, Vivo, Huawei, Samsung; varied OS versions/resolutions) and 2 iOS devices (iPhone 6, iPhone 7). Metric: **Replay Accuracy** = % of successful replays. RQ1 measures overall accuracy; RQ2 compares against a "Widget Feature Matching alone" condition, which the authors argue is representative of state-of-the-art image-matching tools like Sikuli/Airtest since it uses the same underlying algorithm; a module-influence breakdown (labeled bars for cases where Image-only/Layout-only/Final result were correct) quantifies each algorithm's independent contribution; RQ3 is a manual failure-case analysis.
- **challenges:** Quantitative results: overall replay accuracy reaches **65.85% on Android** (an 8.74% relative improvement over Widget-Feature-Matching-alone) and **35.26% on iOS** (a 35% relative improvement, much larger, reflecting how much more Android/iOS renderings of "the same" app diverge visually). Widget Feature Matching alone fails mainly due to (a) repeated highly-similar widgets (~1/3 of its failures, e.g. list items with near-identical thumbnails), (b) parsing/recording-phase errors producing wrong target screenshots (~20%), and (c) too few extractable SIFT feature points on simple/blank widgets. Layout Characterization Matching's own failures are ~63% due to layout-characterization errors (contour segmentation mistakes), ~16.3% due to subtle layout shifts from refreshed/dynamic content, and ~12.1% due to device status-bar differences across devices. Even when *both* individual algorithms fail, 2.47% (Android) / 1.29% (iOS) of final replays still succeed via the merging step, showing the two signals are complementary rather than redundant. Layout Characterization Matching alone drives far more of the final successes (29.59% Android / 91.57% iOS) than Widget Feature Matching alone, especially for cross-platform (Android→iOS) replay where visual widget appearance diverges most. Authors explicitly note the framework does **not** apply to game apps with "no obvious layout." Threats to validity: limited device pool (6 Android + 2 iOS, though chosen to represent mainstream market share), app-category representativeness (games excluded by design), and use of senior SE-major students rather than professional developers/testers as script authors (justified via Salman et al.'s finding that senior students are adequate developer proxies in controlled experiments).

## Summary

LIRAT addresses the "expanded fragmentation problem": as mobile apps proliferate across Android, iOS, mobile web, and app-embedded mini-programs, developers must write and maintain functionally-duplicate test scripts per platform because traditional record-and-replay tools (Instrumentation, Espresso, UIAutomator, KIF, Monkeyrunner, Appium, Sikuli, Airtest, etc.) all rely on platform-specific IDs, XPath locators, or simple whole-image feature matching that cannot survive cross-platform layout/rendering differences or even same-platform content refresh (e.g., a news app's daily-changing thumbnails). The paper's main empirical insight, separately validated on a Google Play sample, is that developers deliberately keep UI layouts highly similar across platform versions of the same app for UX consistency, even though pixel content, exact widget appearance, and underlying implementation differ substantially. LIRAT exploits this via a novel platform-independent script representation (LITS) that records, for every user operation during a "record" pass, the activity screenshot, cropped target-widget screenshot, widget bounding box, operated point, device resolution, on-widget text, and operation type, all subsequently normalized to device-resolution-relative proportions so the script carries no hardware/software dependence. At replay time, two independent CV pipelines vote on the widget's new location: SIFT+FLANN-based Widget Feature Matching (fast, pixel-accurate, but brittle to dynamic/similar content) and Canny-edge-based Layout Characterization Matching (robust to content changes and cross-device rendering differences, but coarser), whose outputs are merged with a tunable weight to yield the final click coordinate. The empirical study across 21 apps and 7 physical devices (5 Android, 2 iOS) shows LIRAT substantially outperforms image-matching-only baselines representative of Sikuli/Airtest, with the improvement being dramatically larger for cross-OS (Android→iOS) replay than same-OS replay, directly supporting the paper's central claim that layout characterization, not pixel matching, is what makes genuine cross-platform script reuse tractable. The paper's own failure analysis is candid about layout characterization's ~63% internal error rate, positioning this as the framework's main remaining bottleneck and future-work direction, and explicitly scopes the approach out for games and other apps lacking a coherent "layout" concept.
