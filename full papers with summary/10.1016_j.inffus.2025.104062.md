## Summary

This is a large-scale systematic review of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a fast-growing class of robotics foundation models that fuse visual perception, natural-language instruction understanding, and low-level robot action generation into one end-to-end multimodal architecture, aiming to push robots past narrow task-specific programming toward generalist, instruction-following autonomy. The authors survey 102 VLA models (RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, CLIPort, Diffusion Policy, Gemini Robotics, and more), 26 training datasets, and 12 simulation platforms, organizing them by fusion hierarchy (early, late, hierarchical), encoder-decoder family, and multimodal integration strategy. Their central contribution is a large quantitative meta-analysis linking these architectural choices, plus a new two-dimensional dataset-complexity framework weighing task complexity against modality richness, to standardized measures of manipulation success and generalization. Hierarchical and late-fusion architectures, along with diffusion-based action decoders, tend to outperform simpler alternatives. The review closes by proposing a "VLA Fusion Evaluation Benchmark" with its own cross-modal alignment and fusion-efficiency metrics, and sketches an "agentic VLA" paradigm where large-language-model planners orchestrate VLA skills as callable tools in a closed perception-action loop. Computer vision, mainly transformer-based visual encoders such as ViT, CLIP, DINOv2, and SigLIP, is foundational to nearly every surveyed architecture, and the review's methodology resembles a standard systematic literature review. But its subject is embodied robotics and multimodal machine learning. None of the 102 surveyed systems addresses any conventional software-engineering lifecycle activity.
