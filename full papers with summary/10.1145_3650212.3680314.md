# VRDSynth: Synthesizing Programs for Multilingual Visually Rich Document Information Extraction

- **Year:** 2024
- **Venue/Source:** ISSTA 2024 (33rd ACM SIGSOFT International Symposium on Software Testing and Analysis)
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3650212.3680314

## Metadata

- **se_area:** N/A in the traditional SE lifecycle sense. Closest mapping is "development" in that the paper contributes a program-synthesis *tool/technique* (published in an SE venue), but the target application domain is document information extraction, not software artifacts
- **se_task:** Program synthesis for semantic entity linking/relation extraction in visually rich documents (forms/invoices), i.e., synthesizing rule-based programs (a domain-specific language over a document graph) that link entities (e.g., header-question-answer relations) based on spatial and textual layout features
- **cv_technique:** Document layout/graph representation derived from visually rich document structure (bounding boxes, spatial relations between text regions), akin to document image analysis; comparison baselines use vision-language document understanding models (LayoutXLM, InfoXLM, XLM-RoBERTa) which are multimodal transformer models pretrained on document images+text+layout
- **platform:** General (document processing; not GUI/mobile/web/desktop software platform in the traditional sense, targeting scanned/digital forms in 8 languages)
- **rationale:** Robustness/precision (program-synthesis approach explicitly optimizes for high precision over the pretrained multilingual document-understanding baselines, and needs no pretraining data); efficiency (drastically lower storage/memory/inference footprint, ~1MB vs. 1.48-2.24GB for baselines, making it deployable without large models)
- **artifact_type:** Full-interface (whole-document graph representation encompassing all entities and their spatial layout relations)
- **eval_method:** Empirical comparison against 3 pretrained multilingual document-understanding baselines (LayoutXLM, InfoXLM, XLM-RoBERTa, base and large variants) on FUNSD (English) and XFUND (8 languages) benchmarks, measuring precision/recall/F1 for semantic entity linking; ablation study (RQ2) on contribution of negative/mutually-exclusive sub-programs; efficiency evaluation (RQ3) measuring inference time and memory/storage footprint
- **challenges:** Recall is generally lower than the pretrained baselines (VRDSynth trades recall for precision); combining VRDSynth with LayoutXLM is needed to get the best F1 in some cases; the DSL/synthesis approach may not represent all possible document structures, especially long-distance or rare relations, limiting generalizability to structures not covered by mined/iteratively-refined patterns; VRDSynth's Python implementation is slower at inference than baselines implemented in optimized C++/PyTorch, despite algorithmic efficiency; the table-extension variant (VRDSynth_Table) is notably less time-efficient than the base variant and than baselines

## Summary

VRDSynth addresses semantic entity linking in visually rich documents (VRDs) such as scanned forms: the task of identifying which text regions on a document (e.g., a form's header, question labels, and answer fields) are semantically related to each other, based on their spatial layout and textual content. Rather than relying on a large pretrained multimodal transformer model (which requires GPU resources and large fine-tuning datasets), the authors propose synthesizing lightweight, precision-oriented rule-based programs expressed in a custom domain-specific language (DSL) that reasons over a document graph (entities and spatial/relational paths between them extracted from the document layout). The synthesis algorithm iteratively refines candidate programs using positive/negative/mutually-exclusive example bindings mined from training documents, with an extension (VRDSynth_Table) that additionally models table structures. On the FUNSD/XFUND multilingual benchmark spanning 8 languages, VRDSynth achieves higher average precision than LayoutXLM, InfoXLM, and XLM-RoBERTa baselines and outperforms LayoutXLM in F1 score on 5/8 languages, while requiring roughly three orders of magnitude less storage (~1MB vs 1.48-2.24GB) and significantly less memory, and being trainable/synthesizable without any pretrained weights. A notable detail is that combining VRDSynth's high-precision output with LayoutXLM's higher-recall output yields the best overall F1 (0.637 average), suggesting the two approaches are complementary. The paper is included in this corpus likely due to its program-synthesis (an SE technique) contribution being demonstrated on a document/visual-layout understanding task at a top software-testing venue, though its core subject matter (document AI, not software GUI/code artifacts) sits at the edge of typical CV-in-SE survey scope, as flagged above.
