# GUIGAN: Learning to Generate GUI Designs Using Generative Adversarial Networks

- **Year:** 2021
- **Venue/Source:** 2021 IEEE/ACM 43rd International Conference on Software Engineering (ICSE)
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ICSE43902.2021.00074

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Design (early-stage/creative GUI design generation, positioned explicitly as assisting the _initial_ stage of GUI design rather than later implementation).
- **se_task:** Automated GUI design generation: producing brand-new, complete mobile app GUI designs by recombining reusable component subtrees mined from existing real-world app screens, intended as inspirational starting points for designers/developers (distinct from GUI _search_/retrieval, GUI _code_ generation, or GUI _implementation_ tasks).
- **cv_technique:** SeqGAN-based generative model (LSTM generator producing sequences of discrete "subtree tokens"; CNN discriminator with highway architecture; Monte Carlo tree search + policy-gradient training since outputs are discrete, non-differentiable subtree choices); a Siamese network (dual-channel, shared-weight CNN, 4 Conv→Pool blocks) trained via binary same-app/different-app classification to learn GUI-subtree "style" embeddings from raw subtree pixel images; homogeneity (HOM) metric computed over these CNN embeddings to score style/aesthetic compatibility of a generated sequence; minimum edit distance (MED) over subtree-structure strings to score structural/layout plausibility; multi-loss fusion (adversarial + style/homogeneity + structural-edit-distance losses, trainable-noise-balanced) drives generator updates. Baselines compared against: WGAN-GP (pixel-based image GAN) and FaceOff (CNN style-compatibility + DOM-tree edit-distance retrieval).
- **platform:** Mobile (Android apps; GUI screenshots and DOM/metadata sourced from the Rico dataset, 72,219 UIs from 9,772 apps across 27 categories, filtered for quality).
- **rationale:** Context-driven (explicitly models GUI component _style compatibility_ and _structural/layout composition rules_, analogous to natural-language sentence generation over subtree "words," rather than treating GUI generation as plain-pixel image synthesis); ease of use / creativity support (aims to lower the GUI design barrier for novice designers and for developers without formal UI/UX training, who otherwise have to search, adopt, and customize existing designs, which raises IP, uniqueness, and design-staleness concerns that the paper explicitly frames as its motivation).
- **artifact_type:** Full-interface: the CV/generative pipeline outputs a complete new mobile GUI screen design, composed and spatially arranged from stitched-together component subtrees, not a localized widget, a temporal sequence, or a natural-input artifact.
- **eval_method:** Automated: Fréchet Inception Distance (FID, via Inception-v3 pool3 features) and 1-Nearest-Neighbor Accuracy (1-NNA) two-sample test, computed separately for GUIs grouped by 5 app categories (News & Magazines, Books & Reference, Shopping, Communication, Travel & Local) and by 3 app-development companies (Google, YinzCam, Raycom), comparing GUIGAN against WGAN-GP, FaceOff, and two ablations (GUIGAN-style: style loss only; GUIGAN-structure: structure loss only). GUIGAN wins on both metrics in both scenarios (33.63%/11.33% and 28.23%/14.18% improvement over best baseline, respectively). Human evaluation: a pilot user study with 5 junior-Android-developer (CS Master's student) raters scoring 10 generated GUIs per category per method (GUIGAN vs. FaceOff) on three 1-5 Likert metrics (design aesthetics, color harmony, structure rationality) plus a binary functional-correctness judgment (e.g., is the menu bar correctly placed at top), with significance assessed via Mann-Whitney U tests; a further blind comparison mixed real-world Rico GUIs with GUIGAN-generated ones and found some generated GUIs rated as highly as, or higher than, real designs.
- **challenges:** Fixed/default component sizes in reused subtrees sometimes fail to fit cleanly into new compositions, causing overlapping elements or oversized single components; the two auxiliary losses (style/homogeneity vs. structural edit-distance) can become imbalanced, yielding either style-consistent-but-structurally-poor layouts or well-structured-but-color-incompatible layouts; user-study participants wanted richer content, which tends to conflict with layout simplicity; the model does not explicitly model app functionality, though it appears to implicitly pick up functionally sensible component placement (e.g., top menu bars) from training data, with functionality-check scores varying notably by app category (0.90 for Travel & Local vs. 0.74 for Books & Reference); participants also requested manual controls over result diversity/style/structure that the current system lacks.

## Summary

GUIGAN targets the earliest, most creativity-demanding stage of mobile GUI design, producing brand-new screen designs, by reframing it as a discrete sequence-generation problem over reusable GUI component subtrees, rather than pixel-level image synthesis. Screenshots (with DOM metadata) are first segmented into first-level subtrees (splitting further if a subtree exceeds 90% of screen width), yielding a repository of 41,813 candidate subtrees from 12,230 GUIs across 1,609 apps. A SeqGAN-style architecture (LSTM generator, highway-CNN discriminator, MCTS/policy-gradient training) then learns to compose new GUIs as ordered sequences of these subtrees. Two auxiliary CV-driven losses steer the generator beyond plain adversarial signal: a Siamese-CNN-derived "homogeneity" score rewards stylistically compatible component combinations (learned by discriminating same-app vs. cross-app subtree pairs), and a minimum-edit-distance loss over subtree-structure strings rewards plausible layout structure. Automated evaluation (FID, 1-NNA) across app-category and app-company scenarios shows GUIGAN substantially outperforming a pixel-based WGAN-GP baseline and a tree-retrieval baseline (FaceOff), and ablations confirm both auxiliary losses independently contribute (style-only and structure-only variants each underperform the full model on their non-optimized dimension). A 5-participant expert user study corroborates the automated results, showing GUIGAN-generated designs rated significantly higher than FaceOff on aesthetics, color harmony, and structural rationality, with some generated designs rated comparably to real-world GUIs in a blind comparison. Participants still flagged component-fit and diversity-control issues as open problems.
