# Generating Minimalist Adversarial Perturbations to Test Object-Detection Models: An Adaptive Multi-Metric Evolutionary Search Approach

> **SCOPE WARNING**: This paper is about *testing computer-vision (object-detection) models themselves* using a search-based/evolutionary SE technique (adversarial test generation), meaning SE methods applied to CV, which is the **inverse** of this survey's stated direction (CV techniques applied to SE tasks). CV here is the System/Model Under Test, not a technique used to accomplish an SE task. There is no "se_task performed via CV" in this paper; rather, the "SE task" (test generation for DL model robustness) is performed via a genetic algorithm, and CV models are only the target of testing. ## Summary

This paper introduces Triple-Metric EvoAttack (TM-EVO), a black-box, search-based testing technique that generates adversarial (minimally perturbed) test images to expose robustness weaknesses in object-detection deep-learning models. It extends the prior EvoAttack genetic-algorithm approach by adding a triple-metric fitness function that jointly considers (1) the average detection confidence of objects in the candidate image (to drive misdetection), (2) the fraction of modified pixels relative to the total (L0-related sparsity), and (3) the normalized Euclidean distance from the original image (L2-related magnitude). A plateau-based adaptation mechanism dynamically reweights these three metrics and adjusts a noise-reduction operator when the search stalls, gradually trading off attack strength against perturbation minimality. TM-EVO is evaluated against the EvoAttack baseline on two object-detection architectures, DETR (Transformer-based) and Faster R-CNN (CNN-based), using images from the COCO and KITTI datasets. Results show TM-EVO reduces L0 norm (visible pixel changes) by 60.8% on average compared to EvoAttack, with statistically significant improvement (Wilcoxon rank-sum, 95% confidence), while L2 norm results are comparable to or modestly better than the baseline, and run times are roughly on par. The effect is most pronounced for the CNN-based Faster R-CNN, where TM-EVO improves both noise metrics substantially, whereas gains for the Transformer-based DETR are more limited, suggesting architecture-dependent susceptibility to minimal perturbations. Notably, the technique's target, computer vision object-detection models, is the object under test, not a means of performing a software-engineering task, which is the reverse relationship this SLR is focused on.
