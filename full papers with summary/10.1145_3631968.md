# Automated Mapping of Adaptive App GUIs from Phones to TVs

> **SCOPE WARNING**: This paper does not use computer vision. The GUI "recognition" pipeline is entirely metadata/DOM-tree-driven (parsing the Android UI Automator accessibility hierarchy: component type, bounding-box coordinates, class, and text taken directly from structured metadata, not extracted from pixels), plus a pre-trained BERT model used only for semantic text similarity between Android activity names (an NLP application, not vision), and a Z3 SMT solver / OR-constraints formulation for layout optimization. Screenshots are captured alongside the metadata but are explicitly stated to play no role in the grouping/conversion algorithm itself (the paper's own evaluation converts images to plain colored blocks in wireframes specifically "since we are comparing the structure of the rendered GUI, not the pixels"). The only computer-vision-technique mention in the entire paper is a single related-work citation (Moran et al.) describing a *different* paper's use of image-similarity CV methods to detect GUI-design violations; the present paper's own method never touches pixels. It is included here only because it was present in the provided reference list; it should likely be excluded from or heavily caveated in the final CV-in-SE synthesis.

## Metadata

- **se_area:** development (cross-platform GUI adaptation / semi-automated code generation tooling)
- **se_task:** semi-automated conversion of an existing Android phone app's GUI into an adaptive, appropriately laid-out Android TV GUI (and generated TV source code), addressing the fact that only ~5% of popular apps support TV displays and that responsive-design and screen-mirroring approaches produce poor TV layouts
- **cv_technique:** none used by the paper's own method; it is a purely metadata/rule-and-constraint-based pipeline built on the Android UI Automator accessibility/DOM-tree hierarchy (component types, bounding boxes, hierarchical relationships), a pre-trained BERT model applied only to activity-name text for semantic GUI pairing, hand-derived heuristic rules (from a formative empirical study) for grouping and re-templating GUI components, an OR-constraints layout-optimization formula solved with the Z3 SMT solver, and a custom GUI Domain-Specific Language (DSL) compiled to real TV code; the only appearance of an actual CV technique (mIoU / Intersection-over-Union) is borrowed purely as an evaluation metric applied to abstracted red/green wireframe blocks representing component structure, not to any visual/pixel analysis
- **platform:** mobile-to-TV (Android phone GUIs converted to Android TV GUIs)
- **rationale:** ease of use / development-cost reduction (motivated by the high resource cost of building and maintaining a separate TV app, and by the poor UX of existing responsive-design and screen-mirroring alternatives) and context-driven (uses an empirical study of 589 real-world phone-TV GUI pairs to derive component-grouping categories and conversion rules that reflect actual TV design conventions, e.g., card-like grid layouts)
- **artifact_type:** full-interface (whole-screen GUI layout conversion, operating over the complete component hierarchy of a page rather than any localized visual feature)
- **eval_method:** a large-scale empirical study (5,580 crawled Google Play apps to determine TV-display support rates; 1,405 collected TV-phone app pairs; 589 manually-verified TV-phone GUI pairs used to derive and validate 12/9 GUI group categories and mapping rules); an accuracy evaluation of the grouping algorithm on 100 held-out GUI pages from 10 new apps against a prior heuristic baseline (Zhang et al.), measured via exact-match rate and isolated-component-reduction rate with Fleiss Kappa inter-rater agreement; an automated accuracy evaluation of the full conversion pipeline on all 589 pairs using mIoU against redesigned ground-truth TV GUIs, compared against desktop-mode and direct-mapping baselines; a 20-participant user study rating structure rationality and overall satisfaction (Mann-Whitney U tests, p<0.01 improvements over both baselines); and a separate usefulness study with 6 professional developers (3 using the tool vs. 3 designing from scratch) measuring completion time and independently-rated satisfaction (tool users: 11.56 min / 3.35 satisfaction vs. control: 16.28 min / 1.66 satisfaction, both p<0.01).
- **challenges:** the approach's grouping and conversion accuracy is fundamentally bounded by the completeness and correctness of the UI Automator metadata it depends on. The paper documents a specific failure case where UI Automator returns confusing/incorrect hierarchy information for non-standard components (an off-screen promotional image in a Bottom Tab Layout), causing the tool to fail that component's conversion; heuristic-rule-based grouping and template matching have inherent generalization limits and cannot handle highly non-standard or app-specific GUI layouts (falling back to a generic grid-layout template when no group type is confidently matched); UI Automator may not correctly capture metadata for certain third-party GUI frameworks and WebView-based views, an acknowledged internal-validity limitation; the tool is explicitly reported by study participants as unsuitable for real-time-demanding apps like games, since generated GUIs must be re-rendered by a client app on the TV; images captured from a small mobile screen sometimes appear visually stretched or degraded once mapped onto a large TV screen, since they were never designed for that display; the method's generalizability rests on heuristic rules derived from a specific empirical sample and the authors identify future integration of deep learning as a path to a more generalized approach.

## Summary

This paper addresses a real and under-served software engineering problem: the vast majority of Android apps that work fine on phones display poorly or not at all on smart TVs, and existing solutions (responsive/adaptive layout frameworks, dedicated TV apps, or screen-mirroring tools) each have serious drawbacks in cost, generality, or user experience. The authors first run a large empirical study (crawling thousands of apps, collecting hundreds of matched phone-TV GUI pairs) to characterize how real TV apps restructure their phone counterparts, deriving 12 phone-side and 9 TV-side GUI "group" categories (e.g., Tool Bar, Search, Pic+Info, Channel) along with heuristic mapping rules between them. Building on this analysis, their tool ingests a phone app's GUI purely through its Android UI Automator accessibility metadata, a DOM-tree-like hierarchy of component types, positions, and text, and applies a three-level grouping algorithm (atomic component grouping via spatial-proximity heuristics, row grouping via DOM subtree width, and multi-row grouping via structural similarity) before matching groups to TV-appropriate templates, applying an OR-constraint layout optimizer (solved with Z3) to fit components onto a TV-friendly grid, and finally emitting a platform-agnostic DSL that a TV-side client app renders into real Leanback-based Android TV GUI code. Although the paper is squarely about a computer-vision-adjacent subject (transforming visual interfaces across devices) and evaluates its output using an Intersection-over-Union metric borrowed from image segmentation, the technique itself never actually processes pixels or trained visual features: everything is driven by structured accessibility-tree metadata, hand-derived heuristic rules validated against empirical GUI-pair data, a pre-trained BERT model used only for matching activity names by text semantics, and a constraint solver for layout arrangement. The most notable and genuinely useful contribution is the thorough empirical grounding: the large-scale measurement of how few apps support TV at all, and the systematic derivation and validation (with Fleiss Kappa agreement and accuracy metrics against a real baseline) of GUI-group taxonomies and conversion rules, together with a comparative user study showing the resulting tool measurably speeds up and improves developer-authored TV GUI design relative to working from scratch.
