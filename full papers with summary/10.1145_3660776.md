# Semi-supervised Crowdsourced Test Report Clustering via Screenshot-Text Binding Rules

- **Year:** 2024
- **Venue/Source:** Proceedings of the ACM on Software Engineering (Proc. ACM Softw. Eng.), Vol. 1, Issue FSE, Article 69 (July 2024), presented at ACM International Conference on the Foundations of Software Engineering (FSE 2024)
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3660776

## Metadata

- **se_area:** testing (specifically crowdsourced/mobile app testing, with downstream relevance to maintenance/bug triage)
- **se_task:** Crowdsourced test report clustering (grouping duplicate/similar bug reports, each consisting of an app screenshot plus textual description, to reduce developer review effort)
- **cv_technique:** Canny edge detection (for GUI widget/region boundary extraction); Optical Character Recognition (OCR) (combined with Canny output to refine widget segmentation and remove overlaps); CNN-based widget classification using VGG-16 (to filter blank/invalid regions and to encode widget screenshot content as 4096-dim feature vectors from the fully-connected layer); the extracted widgets are further organized into a hierarchical (Group/Line/Column) GUI layout tree used as the "Structure Feature
- **platform:** mobile (Android apps; crowdsourced testing platform)
- **rationale:** Context-driven and robustness-driven. The authors argue that prior work treats app screenshots as raw pixel sets (SETU) or analyzes only individual-feature semantics (DeepPrior) without exploiting the semantic relationship between screenshots and text, causing biased/imprecise clustering (e.g., visually similar screenshots reporting different bugs, or visually different screenshots reporting the same bug). CV-based widget/structure extraction is used to recover screenshot semantics (structure + content) robustly against pixel-level noise (e.g., dynamically refreshing app content), and this is paired with text features and "semantic binding rules" (MUST-LINK/CANNOT-LINK constraints) to correct clustering bias caused by any single feature, i.e., robustness of the similarity/distance computation is the central motivation, grounded in concrete GUI-testing context (crowdsourced report review).
- **artifact_type:** full-interface (whole app screenshots are processed to build a full-page GUI structure tree) combined with localized (individual segmented GUI widgets are cropped and separately encoded via CNN for the Content Feature)
- **eval_method:** Dataset: a newly constructed, real-world dataset of 847 crowdsourced test reports from 18 commercial mobile apps (across domains such as Finance, System, Reading, Travel, Communication, etc.), collected via a large, real crowdsourced testing platform in China (20,000+ users, 50 countries); ground-truth bug clusters (109 total, 2–17 per app) were manually labeled by three experienced mobile testing experts independently, then reconciled via cross-validation/discussion (duplication ratio 87.13%). Auxiliary model training: TextCNN (Bug Behavior vs. Reproduction Step sentence classifier) fine-tuned on ~4k reports (6:2:2 split, ~98% precision/recall); VGG-16 widget classifier trained on 36k+ labeled widget screenshots across 14 widget types (7:2:1 split, ~90% precision/recall). Baselines: SETU (Wang et al.), DeepPrior (Yu et al.), and the authors' own earlier demo version SC(d); also ablations (Image-only, Text-only, SC minus each of the 4 features) and an "Unspvsd" (no semantic binding rules) variant. Metrics: Precision, Recall, F-measure, Purity, Adjusted Rand Index (ARI), Normalized Mutual Information (NMI), each run over 10 trials to control clustering randomness. Reports 10.49%–200.67% average improvement over baselines across the six metrics (up to ~3000% on ARI due to its near-zero baseline values). A human/real-world-impact study: 6 graduate students split into two groups inspected 29 reports (6 ground-truth bugs) for one app, with vs. without clustering guidance; the clustering-aided group reviewed only ~6 representative reports (119s avg.) vs. all 29 (584s avg.) while finding a comparable number of bugs (5–6 vs. 5–6), demonstrating practical review-time savings without loss of bug-detection coverage.
- **challenges:** Authors' stated threats to validity: (1) dataset scope: limited to 18 apps/847 reports, though spanning multiple domains, and clustering randomness is mitigated by averaging over 10 runs; (2) manual labeling of ground-truth bug clusters could introduce subjectivity, mitigated via independent labeling plus cross-validation by three experts; (3) the dataset is predominantly Chinese-language (mixed with English), which the authors argue is unlikely to bias comparisons since baselines were validated on similarly mixed-language data, and claim the approach is largely language-agnostic. Not explicitly discussed but reasoned limitations (inferred from experimental setup): the CV pipeline (Canny + OCR + VGG-16) relies on several hand-tuned thresholds (e.g., φ=0.05 for line/column merging, IoU λ=0.75 for widget matching, and the MUST/CANNOT-LINK rule thresholds α, θ, β, ω) that were set via small-scale trials rather than systematically optimized, raising generalizability concerns to apps with very different GUI layouts or widget densities; the widget/structure extraction assumes traditional native Android-style widget layouts and may not generalize well to highly custom-rendered UIs (e.g., games, canvas-based apps, complex WebViews); and the approach assumes exactly one screenshot per report, which may not hold for reports with no screenshot or multiple screenshots.

## Summary

This paper presents SemCluster, a semi-supervised approach for clustering duplicate crowdsourced mobile app test reports, each consisting of one app screenshot and a textual bug description. Unlike prior work that treats screenshots as raw pixel sets or textual descriptions in isolation, SemCluster extracts four semantically grounded features: a Structure Feature (a hierarchical Group/Line/Column GUI layout tree built from CV-based widget detection using Canny edge detection plus OCR, refined by a VGG-16 CNN classifier that filters non-widget regions), a Content Feature (variable-length sets of CNN-derived 4096-dim vectors representing individual widget screenshots), a Bug Behavior feature (Word2Vec-encoded description of the observed/expected app behavior), and a Reproduction Step feature (a formalized operation sequence extracted via constituent parsing, compared with Dynamic Time Warping). Feature-specific distance metrics (tree edit distance, widget-matching Euclidean distance, textual Euclidean distance, DTW) are combined into a weighted "SemDistance." The main contribution is a set of "semantic binding rules" (MUST-LINK / CANNOT-LINK constraints derived from cross-feature relationships between screenshots and text) that guide a K-Medoids clustering process to correct biases from any single feature, illustrated by motivating cases where visually similar screenshots describe unrelated bugs, or dissimilar screenshots describe the same bug. On a newly built 847-report, 18-app dataset, SemCluster outperformed SETU and DeepPrior baselines by 10.49%–200.67% across six clustering metrics, with ablation and a small human study (6 participants) showing both the multi-feature design and the binding rules meaningfully improve accuracy and reduce report-review time in practice.
