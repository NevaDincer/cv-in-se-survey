# Validating Android Interfaces with Faster R-CNN Enhancements in Software Quality Assurance

- **Year:** 2025
- **Venue/Source:** 2025 12th International Conference on Soft Computing & Machine Intelligence (ISCMI)
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ISCMI67495.2025.11358621

## Metadata

- **se_area:** testing
- **se_task:** GUI/screenshot validation for test automation (Android build/release compatibility verification)
- **cv_technique:** Faster R-CNN (Region Proposal Network + ResNet-50 backbone) for object detection, trained separately with Adam, SGD, and RMSprop optimizers
- **platform:** mobile
- **rationale:** context-driven, robustness
- **artifact_type:** localized (bounding-box detection of specific GUI elements/icons within screenshots)
- **eval_method:** Precision, Recall, F1-Score, and mAP50 computed per model on held-out test splits; training/validation loss curves tracked across epochs; comparison across three optimizers and seven dataset-specific models
- **challenges:** Small-object detection difficulty (Google Folder model, no cropping/augmentation applied, worst precision/mAP50 at 54.05%/62.11%); dataset splitting into 7 dedicated models increases computational/training cost and maintenance overhead; proprietary dataset and code unavailable due to NDA with industry partner; hardware limitations caused inconsistent use of early stopping across models; RMSprop underperformed and was excluded from reported results.

## Summary

This paper applies Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-50 backbone to automate visual validation of Android home-screen, browser, notification-bar, and Google Folder layouts as part of Google Mobile Services (GMS) compatibility testing, conducted by Sidia Science and Technology Institute in partnership with Samsung. Rather than training a single detector, the authors partitioned a private dataset of 7,188 approved-test screenshots into seven task-specific models (Notification Bar, Browser, Assist, Home, Google Folder, Home Center/Right, Home Left), each trained on COCO-pretrained weights and evaluated using precision, recall, F1-score, and mAP50. Three optimizers (Adam, SGD, RMSprop) were compared, with SGD proving most effective for five of seven models; RMSprop was dropped from analysis due to poor performance. Six of seven models achieved F1-scores above 90%, with the Home Left model reaching perfect scores, while the Google Folder model struggled (54% precision, 62% mAP50) due to lack of cropping/augmentation and difficulty detecting small, densely packed icons. The study argues that dedicated per-task models ease targeted maintenance and isolate failures, at the cost of higher training/computation overhead versus a unified model. The authors position this work as advancing test automation for Android release approval, contributing empirical evidence on backbone/optimizer choice and dataset partitioning strategy, and propose future integration of OCR-based text recognition and more robust rule validation for the Google approval testing pipeline. Source code and dataset are withheld under NDA.
