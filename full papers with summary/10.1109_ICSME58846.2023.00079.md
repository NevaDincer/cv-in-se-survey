# Engineering Accessible Software

- **Year:** 2023
- **Venue/Source:** ICSME 2023 (IEEE International Conference on Software Maintenance and Evolution), Doctoral Symposium / research-agenda paper
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ICSME58846.2023.00079

## Metadata

- **se_area:** testing (accessibility testing), positioned as part of a broader software-engineering-for-accessibility research agenda spanning design and development tooling as well
- **se_task:** automated detection of motor-impairment accessibility guideline violations in mobile app UIs, specifically touch target size, expanding-section closure, persisting elements, and adjacent icon distance
- **cv_technique:** edge detection (via the UIED tool) for touch-target boundary extraction; FRCNN-based icon detection; GloVe word embeddings + similarity for icon identity matching across screens (used in the persisting-element detector)
- **platform:** mobile (Android)
- **rationale:** context-driven (each detector is purpose-built around a specific accessibility guideline rather than a generic recognizer); ease of use (produces a consolidated, developer-facing "accessibility report" requiring no manual annotation)
- **artifact_type:** localized (individual UI element / icon-level detection, touch targets, icons, expandable sections, rather than whole-screen classification)
- **eval_method:** precision, F1-score, accuracy, sensitivity, and recall per detector, evaluated against MOTORCHECK, a purpose-built benchmark of 484 motor-impairment violation instances; confusion matrices per detector to separately analyze false positive/negative rates (RQ5)
- **challenges:** heavy dependency on uiautomator XML metadata: elements lacking an explicit "clickable" label in the XML are invisible to the touch-target and icon-distance detectors, and dynamically generated screenshots may lack consistent metadata; the expanding-section detector's 23 false positives stem from limitations in lexical pattern matching; the persisting-element detector's false positives arise from inconsistent element matching across screens due to unexpected changes in the XML between states; overall the tool's accuracy is bounded by the accuracy of the underlying UIED element-bound detector it builds on.

## Summary

This ICSME 2023 paper (a doctoral-symposium-style research overview from George Mason University) lays out a research agenda at the intersection of ML, software engineering, and HCI aimed at helping developers build more accessible software, and presents MOTOREASE as its flagship implemented contribution. MOTOREASE is an automated pipeline that runs an Automated Input Generation (AIG) tool over a target Android app to collect paired screenshots and uiautomator XML metadata before/after each action, then applies four independent "violation detectors," for touch-target size, expanding-section closure, persisting-element placement, and adjacent-icon distance, each combining computer vision (edge detection via UIED, FRCNN icon detection, GloVe-embedding-based icon similarity matching) with the structured XML metadata. Detector outputs are compiled into a developer-facing accessibility report. To evaluate the approach, the authors built MOTORCHECK, the first large-scale benchmark of motor-impairment accessibility violations (484 instances), and report per-detector precision/recall/F1/accuracy, with the touch-target detector achieving perfect precision (though a low 0.66 sensitivity) and the icon-distance detector achieving a flawless recall. A notable point of interest for the survey is the paper's explicit framing of future work: a planned search engine that lets developers input prototype screens and receive visually and functionally analogous, more-accessible design alternatives, extending CV-based accessibility support from detection into proactive design recommendation. The paper is short (research-agenda format) and covers only motor-impairment accessibility, explicitly noting DHH and other disability categories remain comparatively underexplored in the literature it surveys.
