# Towards Automatic Oracle Prediction for AR Testing: Assessing Virtual Object Placement Quality under Real-World Scenes

- **Year:** 2024
- **Venue/Source:** ISSTA '24 (33rd ACM SIGSOFT International Symposium on Software Testing and Analysis), Vienna, Austria
- **DOI:** 10.1145/3650212.3680315

## Metadata

- **se_area:** Testing
- **se_task:** Automated test oracle generation for Augmented Reality (AR) apps, specifically detecting imprecise virtual object placement (e.g., floating, sinking, misrotated, or wall-embedded objects) as an automatic pass/fail oracle for AR app testing
- **cv_technique:** Convolutional Neural Network (CNN, custom shallow architecture with one 3×3 convolution layer plus dense layers) combined with a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) in a hybrid multimodal classifier; compared against ResNet as a baseline image-recognition model
- **platform:** Mobile (Android AR apps, primarily built on Google ARCore/Sceneform, including some Unity-based apps)
- **rationale:** Context-driven (the CNN/MLP hybrid model reasons jointly about the screenshot image and runtime scene metadata (object/camera translation, rotation, distance, light condition) to judge whether placement is realistic given the surrounding real-world surfaces and lighting); Robustness (aims to replace subjective, effort-intensive human visual inspection with a consistent automated oracle, validated across random, cross-object, cross-app, cross-scene, and cross-mutation splits)
- **artifact_type:** Full-interface (the CNN analyzes the entire AR camera screenshot, i.e., the whole rendered app view, to assess whether the virtual object is realistically integrated into the scene, rather than a cropped/localized region alone)
- **eval_method:** Custom dataset of 3,043 labeled screenshots (104 distinct virtual objects across 21 real-world AR apps), collected via app instrumentation (APK decompilation to smali, hooking ARCore's hitTest()/createAnchor() APIs) and synthetic position/rotation mutations (±5–15cm floating/sinking, ±15–30° rotation) to balance the dataset; ground-truth labels crowdsourced via Amazon Mechanical Turk (5 workers per screenshot, ratings from -2 to 2 averaged into 4-class labels); evaluated with accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 under five configurations: random split (99.34% acc / 96.92% prec / 100% recall), cross-object (96.95%/95.65%/91.67%), cross-app (95.21%/92.13%/83.86%), cross-scene (91.17%/89.38%/65.80%), and cross-mutation (positional and rotational, ~97-99% acc); compared against a ResNet baseline (89.47% acc, 100% prec, 49.21% recall) which VOPA outperformed by 9.87% (F1), and against a regression-model variant (52.63% acc) to justify the classification framing; additionally validated by manually confirming 38 of 42 real (non-mutated) placement bugs detected in the test sets across configurations
- **challenges:** Authors report two explicit limitations: (1) VOPA operates at the static screenshot level and does not capture the dynamic nature of real user AR sessions; some placement anomalies (e.g., depending on viewing angle/distance changes over time) may be more apparent in video than in single frames, and they plan future work on video-based assessment; (2) the approach is currently tied to apps built with Google ARCore and has not been evaluated on other AR frameworks (ARKit, Wikitude, Vuforia), which may have different underlying scene representations. Authors also note threats to validity: crowdsourced (MTurk) labels carry subjective/labeler bias (mitigated by 5-worker averaging), and the app dataset is limited to 21 apps due to difficulties instrumenting Unity/IL2CPP-compiled apps and excluding non-free or non-interactive AR apps.

## Summary

VOPA (Virtual Object Placement Assessment) is a technique for automatically generating test oracles for Augmented Reality (AR) apps by predicting whether a virtual object's placement in a real-world camera scene looks realistic or is buggy (e.g., floating above a surface, sinking into it, tilted incorrectly, or embedded in a wall). The approach instruments Android AR apps at the smali (decompiled APK) level to hook ARCore's object-placement APIs, extracting runtime metadata (object/camera pose, distance, lighting) and injecting controlled position/rotation mutations to create a balanced set of correct and incorrect placements across diverse real-world surfaces and lighting conditions. Screenshots paired with this metadata are crowd-labeled on Amazon Mechanical Turk into a 4-level realism scale, then used to train a hybrid multimodal classifier that fuses CNN-derived image features with MLP-derived metadata features before a softmax classification head. The main contribution is demonstrating that a real-world (non-simulated) AR test oracle is feasible and outperforms a strong image-only ResNet baseline, while generalizing reasonably well across unseen objects, apps, scenes, and mutation types. A distinctive detail is that VOPA was applied to 21 real, unmodified production AR apps and successfully flagged 38 of 42 genuine (non-synthetic) placement bugs, including objects hovering between two tables or appearing embedded in walls, showing practical bug-finding value beyond the synthetic mutation benchmark.
