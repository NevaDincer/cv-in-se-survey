# Test Automation with Grad-CAM Heatmaps – A Future Pipe Segment in MLOps for Vision AI?

- **Year:** 2021
- **Venue/Source:** 2021 IEEE International Conference on Software Testing, Verification and Validation Workshops (ICSTW)
- **DOI:** 10.1109/ICSTW52544.2021.00039

## Metadata

- **se_area:** testing (with explicit forward links to maintenance/monitoring via MLOps)
- **se_task:** ML model test automation for image-recognition/perception models; bias detection in trained CNNs; explainability-as-test-oracle for classification decisions
- **cv_technique:** Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) for post-hoc visual explanation of CNN activations; underlying CNN is a pruned VGG16-derived architecture (~3.3M parameters, 4 conv layers), with additional validation on pretrained VGG16, VGG19, and ResNet50
- **platform:** general / cyber-physical systems context (proof-of-concept on a static pedestrian-underpass surveillance camera, explicitly framed as a stepping stone toward forward-facing automotive ADAS cameras)
- **rationale:** context-driven (heatmaps reveal whether the model attends to semantically relevant pixels vs. spurious background context, e.g. water puddles, tunnel walls); robustness (surfacing biased/brittle models before deployment); ease of use (heatmaps are argued to be interpretable by non-expert auditors, supporting regulatory/ALTAI compliance)
- **artifact_type:** localized (per-image activation heatmap overlaid on the original frame) plus an aggregate variant: a "combined Grad-CAM" averaging activations across the entire test set, giving a dataset-level (statistical) artifact rather than a single-instance one
- **eval_method:** primarily qualitative/manual. Researchers visually inspect heatmaps for correct and incorrect classifications and cross-reference against manually re-inspected training images to explain activation patterns; the only quantitative measure reported is baseline classification accuracy (80% on the in-domain daytime dataset); no automated or ground-truth-based heatmap scoring metric is used, though the paper proposes future comparison against annotated bounding boxes
- **challenges:** very small, manually annotated training dataset (904 pedestrian, 180 dog-walker, 253 bicyclist, 904 empty images) limiting confidence that findings generalize; authors flag (but ultimately downplay) the risk that heatmaps become less interpretable as CNNs are trained on much larger datasets; current work is restricted to image-recognition (classification) models and does not yet extend to object-detection architectures (YOLO/R-CNN) despite this being identified as necessary future work; automated heatmap-to-bounding-box comparison for use as an actual pass/fail test oracle is only proposed, not implemented

## Summary

This ICSTW workshop paper proposes using Grad-CAM as a building block for testing the explainability and bias of CNN-based perception models within an MLOps pipeline. Using a previously trained, pruned VGG16 classifier for a pedestrian-underpass camera in Helsingborg, Sweden, the authors generate per-image and dataset-averaged Grad-CAM heatmaps for correctly and incorrectly classified pedestrians, cyclists, and dog walkers. They show several concrete cases of the model relying on spurious cues, for example misclassifying a nighttime pedestrian as a "bicyclist" because the model had learned to associate dark curb-side water puddles (common in daytime bicyclist training images) with the bicyclist class, illustrating dataset bias rather than a genuine semantic failure. The paper's main contribution is conceptual: it argues that automated Grad-CAM analysis, compared against ground-truth bounding boxes, could become an automated MLOps test stage that flags models activating on invalid pixels, and it maps this capability onto five of the EU AI-HLEG's seven Trustworthy-AI requirements (human agency/oversight, technical robustness, transparency, fairness, accountability). A notable detail is Figure 9, where a correctly classified pedestrian (98.9% confidence) is nonetheless flagged as a candidate "failed test case" because the activated pixels fall outside the ground-truth bounding box, illustrating how confidence-based metrics alone can mask an unreliable underlying rationale.
